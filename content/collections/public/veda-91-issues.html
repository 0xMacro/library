Expanded Behavior ## [I-1] `requestRedeem` settles in the base asset (BTC.b) and is fulfilled asynchronously The `LombardBTCocDecoderAndSanitizer` sanitizes two functions of the BTCoc "Bitcoin Onchain Credit Strategy" vault: `deposit(address depositAsset, uint256 assets, address receiver)` and `requestRedeem(uint256 shares, address owner)`. Both fund-flow addresses are extracted, and the accompanying Merkle leaves pin `receiver` and `owner` to the BoringVault, so no funds can be misdirected. Two behaviors of the underlying strategy are worth documenting for integrators and downstream accounting: - **Deposits are multi-asset, redemptions are single-asset.** The strategy whitelists both LBTC and BTC.b as deposit assets (`getAssets()`), and leaves are generated for both. Redemptions, however, are always denominated and paid in the strategy's single base asset, BTC.b (`asset()` = `0xB0F70C0bD6FD87dbEb7C10dC692a2a6106817072`), regardless of which asset was deposited. A round-trip therefore leaves the vault holding BTC.b rather than the asset it entered with. - **Redemptions are asynchronous.** `requestRedeem` only burns the shares and records a claim; the underlying payout is pushed later by a BTCoc operator holding `PAY_REDEMPTIONS_ROLE` via `fulfillRedeemRequests`, subject to a one-block minimum delay between request and fulfillment. Until fulfillment, the vault holds a pending claim rather than the asset. Both are the intended design of the strategy. This note is informational so that the vault's accountant is configured to price BTCoc shares and BTC.b, and so that a BTC.b exit route remains available to the strategist after redemption. No vulnerabilities are introduced.
